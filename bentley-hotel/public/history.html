Motherwell House · 1874

The story of the house

Long before it was Bentley Hotel, our building was Motherwell House, a Victorian family home on the High Road.

The house and its street Visit the past

Motherwell House

Home to Motherwell’s founding families

Bentley Hotel stands in a historic building once known as ‘Motherwell House’. Built in 1874, it has been home to many of Motherwell’s founding families.

The story handed down with the house is that it was originally built for Colonel Black of the famous Cameronians. Much has changed inside since then, but the exterior has kept its character: warm sandstone, stepped gables, bay windows and a tall tower.

Today the house holds a locally renowned restaurant, a conservatory and bar, a function suite and 18 en-suite bedrooms. The restaurant sits in the original house, and the conservatory was purpose-built onto the side of it.

We don’t claim to know everything about the house. What we have is the account passed from owner to owner, the building itself, and the records of the town around it. Where the story is a story rather than a documented fact, we say so.

A town of iron and steel

Motherwell in the age of the villa

Motherwell’s name is said to come from the Lady Well, a well dedicated to the Virgin Mary. A plaque on Ladywell Road still marks the spot. It began as a small weaving village, but when the railway arrived in the 1840s the town changed quickly.

Motherwell became a burgh in 1865. David Colville opened his ironworks in 1871, and his Dalzell steelworks began production in 1881. The population rose from just over 700 in 1841 to almost 13,000 by 1881, and the town earned the nickname “Steelopolis”.

The industrialists and professionals who prospered here built their homes close to the town. Many of them chose the area around Hamilton Road and the High Road, where fine sandstone villas went up on land that had been fields and orchards.

Steelmaking shaped the town for more than a century. The Ravenscraig works closed on 24 June 1992 and were demolished in 1996, and the conservation area at our end of the town is now one of the main physical reminders of the industrial years that paid for it.

The Cameronians

The Cameronian name goes back to the 17th-century followers of the Covenanter Richard Cameron, and it was carried by the 26th Cameronian Regiment. In 1881 that regiment became part of The Cameronians (Scottish Rifles), Scotland’s only rifle regiment, which was garrisoned at Hamilton Barracks, just across the Clyde, until 1947. The regiment was disbanded in 1968, and its story is told at the Low Parks Museum in Hamilton.

The Hamilton Road Conservation Area

Motherwell House and its neighbours

Our house stands at the west end of the town, in what North Lanarkshire Council calls the Hamilton Road Conservation Area. The council’s appraisal of the area explains how this corner of Motherwell came to look as it does.

The oldest feature of all is the line of a Roman road, built in the 2nd century AD, which ran up the Clyde Valley through what is now the town centre. For most of the centuries after that, this was farmland. Hamilton Road was called Clyde Street, and the land around it belonged to High Motherwell Farm: open fields, orchards and gardens.

From 1879, plots on the Duke of Hamilton’s estate land began to be feued out, and the town’s growing middle class built detached and semi-detached villas on them. The first plots to be built on lay along Clyde Street, Orchard Street and the east side of Avon Street. The town’s old Town Hall, opened in 1887, and its Carnegie Library were built at the top of what is now Hamilton Road.

Most of the buildings in the area are still those sandstone Victorian and Edwardian villas, of one, two and two-and-a-half storeys, with slate roofs, stone boundary walls and mature trees in the gardens. Within the conservation area, little remains of the Motherwell that existed before the ironworks. The exception is a weaver’s cottage at the corner of Hamilton Road and Avon Street, a trace of the weaving village the town used to be.

By the account handed down with the house, Motherwell House was standing before most of its neighbours were built. Today the conservation area protects the character of the whole group. It was designated in 1993, and when the boundary was revised in 2011 it took in Bentley Hotel, the bowling club beside us, the Heritage Centre and the villas along High Road. The house itself is not a listed building; it’s the street as a whole that the designation looks after.

A walk along Hamilton Road

If you have half an hour, walk up Hamilton Road from the hotel and look for the villas’ stone boundary walls, the bay windows and towers, the weaver’s cottage at Avon Street and the old Town Hall at the top. The council’s appraisal notes that the plots get bigger and the architecture bolder as the road rises towards the town centre.

Through the years

A short timeline

  1. 1840s

    The railway reaches Motherwell

    The railway transforms a small weaving village into a growing industrial town.

  2. 1865

    Motherwell becomes a burgh

    The town gains burgh status as industry and population grow.

  3. 1871

    Colville’s ironworks

    David Colville opens his ironworks. His Dalzell steelworks follows in 1881.

  4. 1874

    Motherwell House is built

    The house that is now Bentley Hotel is built, as the story goes, for Colonel Black of the Cameronians.

  5. 1879

    Villas on the farmland

    Feuing of plots in the town’s west end begins on the Duke of Hamilton’s estate land, and sandstone villas rise on the fields and orchards of High Motherwell Farm.

  6. 1881

    The Cameronians (Scottish Rifles)

    The 26th Cameronian Regiment becomes part of The Cameronians (Scottish Rifles), garrisoned at nearby Hamilton. The same year, Motherwell’s population passes 12,900.

  7. 1887

    The old Town Hall opens

    Motherwell’s Town Hall opens on Clyde Street, now Hamilton Road, in the town’s newly built-up west end.

  8. 1992

    The end of an era

    Ravenscraig steelworks closes on 24 June, a little over 120 years after David Colville opened his ironworks in the town.

  9. 1993

    A conservation area

    Motherwell’s Hamilton Road Conservation Area is designated to protect the town’s historic villas.

  10. 1996

    The Heritage Centre opens next door

    Motherwell Heritage Centre, now the North Lanarkshire Heritage Centre, opens on High Road beside the house. The same year, the Ravenscraig works are demolished.

  11. 2011

    Bentley Hotel joins the conservation area

    The conservation area boundary is revised to include Bentley Hotel, the bowling club, the Heritage Centre and the traditional villas along High Road.

  12. Today

    Bentley Hotel

    Eighteen en-suite bedrooms, a restaurant and conservatory, a bar and a function suite for up to 120 guests.

Where to find out more

Staying with us puts you next door to the area’s archives, and a short journey from the museum that tells the Cameronians’ story.

Next door

North Lanarkshire Heritage Centre

Opened as Motherwell Heritage Centre in 1996, and right next door to us on High Road. Admission is free. Its exhibitions trace the area’s history from Roman times, its viewing tower looks out over the Clyde Valley, and it houses North Lanarkshire’s archives, the place to start if you want to dig into the past of the house or the street.

In Hamilton

Low Parks Museum

Across the Clyde in Hamilton, Low Parks Museum tells the story of The Cameronians (Scottish Rifles), the regiment our house’s story is tied to. The regiment was garrisoned at Hamilton Barracks from 1881 to 1947 and disbanded in 1968. Ask us for directions before you set out.

About a mile

The Roman bathhouse

The Romans were here long before the weavers or the steelmen. The remains of a Roman bathhouse survive in Strathclyde Country Park, about a mile from the hotel, and the Heritage Centre next door picks the story up from there.

Questions about the house

Is Bentley Hotel a listed building?

No. The house is in the Hamilton Road Conservation Area, which North Lanarkshire Council designated in 1993 and extended in 2011 to include the hotel, the bowling club, the Heritage Centre and the villas along High Road. A conservation area protects the character of the street as a whole; the house itself is not listed.

Who was Colonel Black?

The story handed down with the house is that it was built in 1874 for Colonel Black of the Cameronians. We have not yet traced an independent record of him, which is one reason we’d love to hear from anyone with documents or family stories about the house. If you can help, please get in touch.

Can I still see the original house?

From the outside, yes. The sandstone, the stepped gables, the bay windows and the tall tower are all there to see from High Road. Inside, much has changed over 150 years of family life and hotel-keeping, so we don’t promise period rooms. The restaurant is in the original house, and the conservatory was added later, purpose-built onto the side.

Why is the function suite called the Back o’ Barns room?

We’d love to know for certain. If you can tell us how the room came by its name, please get in touch and we’ll add the story here.

Where can I find out more about Motherwell’s history?

Start next door at the North Lanarkshire Heritage Centre, which is free and holds the area’s archives. For the Cameronians, visit the Low Parks Museum in Hamilton. For the streets around us, the council’s Hamilton Road conservation area appraisal is a good read.
